03 · How it works

Three small ideas. One shared heartbeat.

Simple enough to explain over coffee. Strong enough to keep a hundred people honest.

Out there›On the air›Decided

A shared pulse

Decisions don't happen on a whim. They wait for the community's pulse — a moment when the group agrees "we're ready, let's decide." Anyone can call the pulse. Nothing moves until enough of you say it's time.

Democratic governance

The rulebook is two things together: statements — plain sentences anyone can point to — and variables, the tunable thresholds. Change either with a proposal. The rules never live in someone's head; they live where everyone can see them.

Working groups that stick

Big projects need small teams. Spin up a working group — anyone can join — and let it write, revise, and wrap up its piece of the work. When it's done, the result comes back to the community for a vote.

04 · Why we built another one

Not a forum. Not a DAO.

Group chats have no decision moment. Forums collect opinions but never resolve them. Voting apps and DAOs count ballots but have no shared time. Kibbutznik brings three mechanics none of them have.

pulse#01

The pulse

A heartbeat where the community says "we're ready, let's decide what we've been building up to." Between pulses you propose and support. At a pulse, anything with enough support becomes rule — all at once, on the beat. No proposal drifts forever: every cycle has a deciding moment.

depth∞

Hierarchical communities

Any community can spin off an action — a nested sub-community with its own pulse, members and rulebook. It does its piece, then commits the result back up to its parent for a vote. Actions can have actions, so a whole movement can be one community of actions of actions.

admins00

No administration. Everything is proposable.

No admin tier, no moderator, no founder veto. Every rule, every threshold, every member's status can change through the same proposal. Even the support threshold is a variable the community can vote to change — the rules about rules included.

05 · Built for the people in the room

Who's it for?

Anywhere a group needs to actually decide things together — not just chat about deciding things together.

Use case 01

co-ops & collectives

Coordinate decisions without committees, vote-counting, or that one person doing the spreadsheet.

Use case 02

online communities

Move beyond mods and karma. Let your members shape the rules they live by.

Use case 03

friends

For when a group chat just isn't enough. Decide trips, budgets and traditions — together.

Use case 04

schools & classrooms

Teach democracy by doing it. A safe place to practise making real choices together.

Use case 05

researchers

Run simulated communities populated by AI agents, and watch how groups settle, split or adapt.

Use case 06

anyone tired of meetings

If you've sat through a 90-minute call about whether to have another call, this one's for you.

Use case ∞

…and eventually, the whole of humanity.

Wherever a few people need to make something together — a building, a town, a treaty. The rules already apply to all of us; Kibbutznik just makes them visible, and lets the group write its own.
